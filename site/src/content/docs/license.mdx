Einsora is open source under the **MIT licence**, and free for everyone
([ADR-0011](../decisions/0011-free-for-everyone/), [ADR-0020](../decisions/0020-name-and-licence/)).

## In plain English

**You may** use Einsora for anything, including commercially; copy it; change it; merge it into your
own software; and publish, distribute, sublicense, or sell copies.

**You must** keep the copyright notice and the licence text in every copy or substantial portion of it.

**There's no warranty.** The software is provided as is: the authors aren't liable for anything that
happens from using it.

This summary helps you read the licence; it doesn't replace it. The licence below is what applies.
</Aside>

## What it covers, and what it doesn't

- **Covered:** the code and documentation in the
  [Einsora repository](https://github.com/krishnanand20/Einsora).
- **Not covered: models.** Each model keeps its own licence, and you must follow it (Llama's, for
  example). The catalog shows a model's licence when it's known.
- **Not covered: dependencies.** Third-party libraries keep their own licences (Apache-2.0, BSD, MIT,
  and similar), all of which allow this use. Their notices ship with Einsora's binaries.
- **Not covered: the name.** The MIT licence grants rights to the software, not to the Einsora name or
  logo.
- **No paid edition.** There's nothing to buy: no paid tier, no enterprise edition, and no licence keys.
